# Provenance and transformation inventory

The specimen generator is synthetic. WATERS 2015 supplies only the explicitly listed distributions and shapes;
it is not an architecture to reconstruct. The domain taxonomy, component/signal/service names,
hierarchy, and demonstration flows are invented by the specimen generator. Nothing claims correspondence to a
real vehicle, ECU, OEM, vendor, customer, or proprietary project. The exact checked source is
Kramer, Ziegenbein, Hamann, “Real World Automotive Benchmarks For Free”, WATERS 2015, Tables I,
III, IV, V, VI, VII and Sections IV-A through IV-E.

| one unrated partition per core, `Partition_<core>` | Specimen generator assumption | Derived from the preset's cores, one `kind: partition` per core with no `asil`, consuming no PRNG draw. Stated limit: with unrated partitions, SWCraft's zero findings no longer prove that a component sits on a core able to host its level, and SWCraft's partition- and memory-level safety checks compare against nothing; only the specimen generator's own placement check (the capability rule) proves the level-to-core fit |

| synthetic scale calibration | Specimen generator assumption | Seeds 2026092101/2/3 and reviewed counts/fingerprints were selected by a bounded feasible search; no source claims these are vehicle measurements |
| planted and displaced placement | Specimen generator assumption | Whole-domain planted packing, pinned private-memory components, and exactly 3/8/20 displaced components provide a deterministic demonstration; small oracle independently checks up to three moves |
| semantic catalog and hierarchy | Specimen generator assumption | Domain taxonomy, semantic identities, compositions, boundary ports, delegations, and demonstration flows are invented and deterministic |
| WATERS usage boundary | Non-claim | WATERS is used only for the listed timing and signal-size distributions/shapes; it does not supply names or topology |

Every key in `swcraft_specimens/presets/small.yaml`, `medium.yaml`, and `large.yaml` is covered above. The exact redesigned production counts are 15/57/23, 60/196/239, and 160/532/1306 for components/runnables/connectors; root direct member counts are 3, 6, and 8; each production preset has 15 scenarios.

## Specimens from a passport

A specimen's figures come from the passport it is generated from; everything else of it is a
specimen generator assumption. No real passport is committed; the real-passport integration test reads
them from a directory outside the repository (`SWCRAFT_SPECIMENS_REAL_PASSPORTS`) and reports figures
only.

## Specimens: the later shapes, ported

The tasks, the sequences, the requirement cases and their texts, the data accesses and the
per-core RAM of a specimen are rules of SWCraft's benchmark generator,
`tools/tests/benchmarks/workload.py` at SWCraft commit `eccd46216` (its last change; 1,585 lines),
**restated** in the specimen generator's own modules. Nothing is
imported from SWCraft and no SWCraft code is vendored (SPEC § 18(b)). The table is provenance, a
record of where each rule comes from and what the specimen generator changes; it is not a licence. The one
verbatim content is the seven requirement topics.

| Piece (specimen generator module) | `workload.py` lines | What the specimen generator restates | What the specimen generator changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| the requirement topics, `REQUIREMENT_TOPICS` (`requirements.py`) | 72–90 | The seven titles and descriptions, **verbatim**, as a generator constant | Taken by the spread cases, then the unallocated ones, case k taking topic k mod 7 (cyclic; `workload.py` allows at most seven cases); the one-port and general requirements take neutral texts; each spread or unallocated case gives the sentence "Takes part in <title in lower case>." to up to three components from the end of the component order that hold no link of it |
| the data accesses (`accesses.py`) | 96, 260–265, 705–718 | Each component's runnable writes every data element of each of its sender-receiver provide ports and reads every data element of each of its sender-receiver require ports, through that port, connected or not; a client-server port gets none | Always written, no switch (`workload.py`'s `data_accesses` knob); the runnable is the component's first, `R1`; a port on an external interface stub gets none, and so does a component with no runnable; every sender-receiver interface has the one element `D1` |
| the tasks, the even split, the timing back-patch and the sequences (`tasks.py`) | 805–845 | The runnables split into contiguous chunks, one per busy task; periods 10 to 50 ms by k mod 5; each mapped runnable gains one `timing` event at its task's period; a sequence of the first min(3, chunk) runnables with an empty note and title | The uneven split (sizes differing by at most one, larger first) where `workload.py` needs divisibility; the partitions laid over whole components in contiguous blocks, not k mod cores; rate-monotonic priorities by period rank, not k + 1; every task `wcet: 1us` on the strict cores; a task with components of several levels writes a `coexistence` text; the sequences go round robin over the busy tasks, an unstated count being one per busy task; the specimen generator's names `Ts…` and `Sq…` |
| idle tasks (`tasks.py`) | 847–858 | Tasks with no runnable at 100 ms | Idle task j names partition j mod the partitions; the priorities are the rate-monotonic ranking's |
| the common requirement and the one-port requirements (`requirements.py`) | 859–885 (and 727–728) | A requirement on every component, and requirements linked on one port each | The common requirement is the first general one, linked on every component when the links leave it that many; the one-port requirement j sits on the port (P − 1 − j) mod P; the other general requirements, the extra links and a derived link count are the specimen generator's; texts as in the topics row above; no `asil`; the specimen generator's names `Rq…` |
| the budgets, the levels and the per-core hardware (`hardware.py`) | 887–932 (the RAM budgets and sizing 897–925) | Component budgets 16,384 >> i for the hubs and 512 × (1 + 7i mod 8) otherwise; a memory sized max(4,096, ⌈C × 10 / 6 / 4,096⌉ × 4,096) for its budgets' sum C, threshold 0.8; one RAM accessible from one core; a partition's `coexistence` text | The budgets go by the specimen generator's component order (descending ports), the first component's unique (16,384 >> i, at least 512, for i < max(hubs, 1)); the RAM's core is taken through the component's partition (its tasks), and memory j is accessible from core j mod cores, so a passport with memories ≠ cores is covered; a rated partition takes the highest effective level its tasks run, not `workload.py`'s fixed levels; the cores keep strict enforcement and threshold 0.7 |
| `_spread_sites` and `_write_requirement_cases`: spread, unallocated and stale requirements (`requirements.py`) | 934–1029 | A spread requirement linked once on each of seven site kinds (component, port, runnable, interface, the root composition, task, hardware); unallocated requirements with no link; stale links stated at the version before, the requirement at version 2 | The sites are the first of each kind by index (s mod the kind's count), not `workload.py`'s fixed offsets that avoid its change tasks; the hardware site is the processor; extra links beyond the least go to the general, then the spread requirements; the stale links are the first V of one enumeration of every link (spread base links, component and port first, then one-port, general and spread extra links), not the component and port links of the last cases |

Not ported: `apply_fixed_mutations` (it stays in SWCraft) and the rule by which a knob the first
generation shows to be needed regenerates the model whole. `workload.py`'s names (`WlTask000`,
`REQ-WL-000`, …) are replaced by the specimen generator's stems.
